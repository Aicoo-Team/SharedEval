# Reply to aP1N follow-up (specificity threshold & production evaluation)

Thank you for the follow-up; it goes to the heart of what our new controls let us answer precisely.

1) We would gently push back on one premise: our new ablations suggest there is no scalar "specificity threshold" to define. Specificity decomposes into components with very different effects. A length-matched control that only restates the generic caution (P9, 323 words) leaves disclosure unchanged versus the 22-word original (80.0% vs 81.5% Files; 62.0% vs 63.0% States; all paired McNemar p ≥ 0.61). Naming the protected categories alone, in 22 words (P7), gets partway (75.0% → 42.5%). The full package at the same 323 words (P2) reaches 7.5%. So the operative variables are discrete: *which categories are named* and *whether operational enforcement is specified* (default-deny, refusal handling, write gating), not how much specificity is present on a single axis.

2) What we believe holds across domains is therefore a component checklist, not a threshold value: (a) the policy explicitly names that domain's protected categories, and (b) it specifies operational enforcement rather than intent. Our category-level results support this: personal categories are refused near-uniformly (90 to 100%) regardless of requester, while work-adjacent items vary by 17 pp with the requester. The calibration burden concentrates in the domain's *ambiguous, institutionally shared* categories, which is exactly the part that must be re-instantiated per domain.

3) On a production-ready evaluation: the procedure our platform instantiates is, we believe, directly deployable, and the only domain-specific inputs are a category list and gold facts. Concretely: (i) write the domain's scenario contracts (requester, relationship, object, operation); (ii) run paired public/protected tasks with dual metrics, including database-diff scoring for write actions; (iii) evaluate candidate policies as discrete operating points along the component ladder above (generic, +categories, +operational rules), so a deployer sees what each component buys in their domain; (iv) verify scoring with judge-free gold-string matching plus a different-family judge; (v) accept or reject against preregistered bounds (for example, disclosure ≤ X at utility ≥ Y for the roles that matter). Steps (i) to (v) run on any MCP-compatible deployment.

4) Scope, stated plainly: our evidence covers one seeded world, four protected categories, and current models. Cross-domain replication of the component pattern is exactly the follow-up the released platform and protocol are designed to enable, and we will state this in the revision.
